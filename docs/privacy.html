LocalIntelligence privacy policy

Effective 2026-11-08. Applies to the LocalIntelligence app on Google Play (local.intelligence) and the full build installed from GitHub (local.intelligence.full).

Who we are

LocalIntelligence is developed by darioo, the developer named on its Google Play listing. Privacy questions: open an issue at github.com/riceharvest/LocalIntelligence/issues, or write to the contact email on the Google Play listing.

The short version

The assistant runs a language model on your phone. The developer receives nothing unless you send a report about a reply. The app has no account, no analytics, no crash reporting and no ads. When a question needs the internet, the assistant searches the web, opens web pages and looks up weather by itself, so those services receive what is listed below. Other data leaves your phone only through features you turn on.

What stays on your phone

  • Your chats, the facts the assistant remembers about you, the "About you" text, scheduled tasks, documents you add, downloaded models and your settings.
  • Keys and passwords you enter for other services. They are encrypted with the Android Keystore and never shown back.
  • Android backup is turned off for the app, so this data is not copied to a cloud backup.

What the app can read on your phone

Only when a request needs it, and only after you grant the Android permission:

  • Contacts and calendar, to look up people and events and create entries you ask for.
  • Text messages and the call log, only while LocalIntelligence is your default digital assistant.
  • Approximate location, for weather and nearby places.
  • The microphone, for dictation, hands-free mode and the optional wake word. Audio is processed on the phone and is not stored or sent anywhere, unless you chose a remote model that takes voice. The exception is dictation on a phone with no on-device speech recognition: it then uses your phone's system speech service, which may send the audio to that service's provider.
  • Notifications (if you allow notification access), screen-time totals, the clipboard, and files you pick.
  • Search my phone, for the sources that are switched on in Settings: notifications from the time you switch them on, texts (only while LocalIntelligence is your default assistant), calendar events, contact names (never numbers or addresses), text files in a folder you choose, and the captions from Photo search. In Simple mode, calendar and contact names are on by default once you have given the app that permission. The matches are stored in the app's own database on your phone. You can delete each source there at any time.
  • Full build only: the screen of other apps while you let the assistant control them, and all files on the phone if you grant "All files access".

When data leaves your phone

Web search, opening web pages, and weather, places and Wikipedia work without being turned on. The assistant uses them when a question needs them, and it may do so without asking first. The rest are off until you use or turn them on. Each service named receives what is listed and handles it under its own privacy policy.

  • Search my phone: the index itself sends nothing anywhere. If a cloud chat model is the one answering, the matches the assistant finds are sent to that model in that chat, the same as any other tool result.
  • Reports you send: the reason, your note, the model name, the app version and, if you leave the box ticked, the reply text, go to the developer through Google Forms (Google's privacy policy applies). Nothing else from your chats is sent.
  • Model downloads from Hugging Face, and skills or plugins from a link you give: the file request.
  • Weather, places and Wikipedia (Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap Nominatim, Wikipedia): the place name or search words the assistant looks up. Weather with no place named sends your phone's location to Open-Meteo instead.
  • Opening a web page: the request to that site, which also sees your IP address, as any website does.
  • Update check (full build only): when you tap "Check for updates" and confirm, a request to GitHub for the latest release's version and notes. GitHub receives it as any web request from your phone. Nothing from your chats is sent.
  • A remote model (OpenRouter, your ChatGPT plan, Groq, Cerebras, DeepSeek, Together, Mistral, Google Gemini, or your own server): the whole conversation, including tool results, screen text in the full build, and attached images or audio.
  • Image and video generation through OpenRouter: the prompt.
  • Web search (on by default, no key): the search words, sent to one service at a time. The services are DuckDuckGo, then Bing, then Brave Search, then Wikipedia in your phone's language. The next one is asked only when the one before it did not answer with results. Each one gets the search words and no account or cookies, and it sees your IP address, as any website does.
  • Search with your own key (Brave, Tavily, Exa, or your SearXNG): the search words.
  • My desktop (full build only, off until you enter an address): your messages and the keys you tap go to the https address you set, which is your own computer over your Tailscale network. Its text is saved on your phone so you can read it offline. Nothing goes to the developer or any other server.
  • Connected services you set up: MCP servers (including Zapier and Composio), cloud sandboxes (Daytona, Vercel, your SSH server), the Browser Use cloud browser, your email provider, your Home Assistant, and Telegram for chats you link. Each receives the requests the assistant makes to it.

Extras (not launched). Extras is a paid option that is not available yet, and nothing described here is sent until it launches. If you buy it, the app sends the Google Play purchase token for that purchase and a random install id, which is not your identity, to the developer's Extras server. The server checks the subscription with Google Play and returns usage-capped access for this phone. Chat goes from your phone directly to OpenRouter and searches directly to Exa. Speech you dictate goes directly to Deepgram, and text read aloud is sent to Deepgram through the Extras server. When the assistant uses the cloud code sandbox, the cloud browser, your connected apps (through Composio), the Extras email address or the Extras Telegram bot, those requests go through the Extras server, which runs on Cloudflare, to that service: code and files you run in the sandbox, the pages the browser visits (the browser's AI steps use your Extras chat allowance through OpenRouter), the actions on apps you connect, the emails sent to and from your Extras address (stored on the Extras server so the app can read them), and the Telegram messages of chats you link (held until your phone collects them). The Extras server keeps only what each service needs and a monthly usage count per service.

How it is protected

  • Keys go over HTTPS only. Plain HTTP is allowed only to a server on your own home network that you entered yourself.
  • In Technical mode, sending messages, placing calls, deleting files and opening websites ask for your approval unless you turn that off per category. Simple mode, the default, does not ask for most of these. In both modes, texts and calls always ask, and so does anything your message did not ask for, apart from web search and opening its results.
  • Text from outside (web pages, servers, other apps' screens) is treated as data, never as instructions to the assistant.

Keeping and deleting data

Everything is kept on your phone until you delete it. Delete chats in the app, or uninstall the app or clear its storage to remove all of it. Crash reports stay on your phone unless you choose to share one from Settings.

A report you send is kept by the developer until the problem is handled, and for at most 12 months, then deleted. To ask for earlier deletion, open an issue at github.com/riceharvest/LocalIntelligence/issues. Data you sent to a third-party service is kept and deleted under that service's policy.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13.
